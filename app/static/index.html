Check your voice and cough for signs of lung disease

Chronic lung diseases such as COPD reduce airflow and breath support. That changes how long you can sustain a sound, how often you pause, how stable your voice is and how your cough sounds. Record the short tasks below and two machine-learning models estimate how closely your recordings resemble those of people with lung disease.

Not a medical device. This is an experimental screening demo trained on small research datasets. It cannot diagnose or rule out any disease. Spirometry is the clinical standard; talk to a doctor about any breathing symptoms.

About the voice model

Data

Trained on voice recordings from the Coswara dataset (Indian Institute of Science), also mirrored on Kaggle. Cases are participants who reported a chronic lung disease; controls reported no respiratory condition or symptom and were matched to each case on sex, age (±10 years) and recording year.

Features & model

Phonation time, pause ratio, pitch statistics, jitter, shimmer, harmonics-to-noise ratio, MFCCs and spectral shape from both recordings, fed to the best of four classifiers chosen by repeated 5-fold cross-validation, then probability-calibrated.

Cross-validated performance

Matched-pair concordance is how often a case scored above its own sex-, age- and year-matched controls, the fairest single number here. Baselines show how much the demographics alone and the recording year alone would predict.

Limitations

  • Small cohort; the diagnosis is self-reported “chronic lung disease”, which includes conditions other than COPD.
  • Recorded on participants’ own phones and browsers in India in 2020–2022; accuracy on other languages, accents and microphones is unknown.
  • The probability is relative to the training mix of cases and controls, not to disease prevalence in the general population.

About the cough model

Data

Trained on the COPD-Cough (CC) dataset (University of Science and Technology of China), with smartphone coughs from people with clinically diagnosed COPD and healthy volunteers.

Ignoring the background

In this dataset the background noise alone predicts COPD, probably because patients and volunteers were recorded in different places. To stop the model from learning the room instead of the cough, each recording is band-limited to 100–4000 Hz, its own noise profile is subtracted, and only the detected coughs are analysed. During training, every cough is also replayed over the background of other recordings, so the background carries no information about the label.

Cross-validated performance

“Background swapped” re-scores every test cough over the background of a recording from the opposite group. A model that relies on the room changes its answer; a model that listens to the cough does not.

Limitations

  • One cohort from China with no age, sex or patient information, so age cannot be matched and the same person may appear in several recordings.
  • Background removal reduces but cannot fully exclude differences in rooms and phones between the groups.
